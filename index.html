Wordforge

Same idea as that word game you know. More letters, more modes, more pressure.

Mode

Rounds

How it works
  1. Every round picks a secret word — 4 or 5 letters, so don't assume it's always five.
  2. Most rounds must start with the last letter of the previous round's word. Watch the chain badge.
  3. Type guesses on your own keyboard — the one on the desk in front of you. There's nothing on screen to click. Each letter pops in as you type it. Tiles flip to show hit (right spot), present (wrong spot) or miss, and a guess that lands zero hits gets its own shake and sting. Any letter you've used that isn't in the word greys out in the legend below the board, so you never waste a guess retyping it.
  4. Every round has a 5-minute clock, ticking audibly in the last 10 seconds. Run out, and the round ends there — revealed, unsolved, for whoever hadn't gotten it yet.
  5. Round-start events: about 2 rounds in 3 draw one, announced across the whole screen for the full 5-second countdown so nobody misses it — 💰 double points, ⚡ a 90-second blitz, 🙈 blackout (the legend stops greying out known letters), and a rare (1 in 20) 🎰 Jackpot: extra guess AND double points, with confetti, a screen shake and a slot-machine fanfare.
  6. Mid-round events: about half of all rounds also spring something on you partway through, out in the room around your monitor rather than on it — 🐈 a cat wanders in (click within 4 seconds for 20 bonus seconds on the clock), or 📱 the phone rings on the desk (answer within 4 seconds for one extra guess). Either way, missing it costs nothing. In Co-op only, a 🔀 Letter Swap can also strike: two guesses (never a winning one) suddenly have their tiles mixed up.
  7. Solo: no lobby, no code — just you against the word, same guess budget as a duel. Good for warming up or chasing a personal best.
  8. PvP Duel: you and a rival race the same word at the same time. You only ever see your own board — the "ghost" bar shows how many guesses your rival has burned, nothing more. Whoever solves it first wins the round, however many guesses it took.
  9. Co-op: the whole room shares one board and one guess budget (word length + 3, no bigger a team just because you invited more people). Anyone can drop the next guess — coordinate or waste attempts.
  10. Points build across the whole match. After the last round, whoever — or whichever team — has the most wins.
